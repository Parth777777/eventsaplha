Alpha score (0–100)

The alpha score is a regime-aware composite that combines historical event-outcome hit rate, current market regime, magnitude calibration, and source credibility. It is computed at signal-publication time and does not change after the fact.

  • Event hit rate — base rate of similar prior events resolving in the predicted direction.
  • Source credibility — the source's own historical hit rate (lower-tail sources are dampened).
  • Magnitude calibration — predicted return scaled by recent realised volatility of the underlying ticker.
  • Market regime — bull / bear / sideways / crisis adjustment to suppress contra-trend signals.

Conviction tiers

  • Strong signal · alpha ≥ 80 — high historical hit rate + favourable regime
  • Moderate signal · 60 ≤ alpha < 80 — meaningful base rate
  • Watchlist · alpha < 60 — informational only

Tier labels were softened (from High / Moderate / Watch) in v1.0 to reflect their analytic, non-recommendation nature.

Reference levels (entry / target / horizon)

"Reference entry" and "indicative level" values on signal cards are statistical extrapolations from prior similar events — not analyst price targets, not trade recommendations. They exist so you can compare current price against the historical event response, not as instructions to transact.

Known limitations

  • Hit rates with n < 3 are suppressed (insufficient sample).
  • Earnings whisper relies on consensus EPS — missing for many mid/small caps.
  • Concall sentiment is best-effort on PDFs > 72 hours old.
  • BSE-only stocks may have sparser fundamental coverage than dual-listed peers.
  • Backtests do not adjust for slippage, taxes (STT/STCG), or brokerage.
