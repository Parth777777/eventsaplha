What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is the practice of investing a fixed rupee amount in a mutual fund every month (or any chosen interval). Each contribution buys units at the prevailing NAV — so you average your cost across market highs and lows. Over a long enough horizon, SIPs let compounding do the heavy lifting: the returns earned in earlier years themselves earn returns in later years.

Equity-oriented mutual funds in India have historically delivered 10-15% CAGR over 15-20 year periods, though year-to-year returns are volatile. The SIP calculator above assumes a constant return; real returns will fluctuate, but the long-term math is a useful guide for planning.

How the SIP formula works

The future value of a monthly SIP compounded monthly is:

FV = P × [ ((1 + r)n − 1) / r ] × (1 + r)

Where:

  • P = monthly investment in rupees
  • r = monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12 (e.g. 12% / 12 = 1% = 0.01)
  • n = total months (years × 12)

Example: ₹10,000 per month for 15 years at 12% per annum = 10000 × ((1.01^180 − 1) / 0.01) × 1.01 ≈ ₹50.46 lakh. You invested ₹18 lakh; ₹32.46 lakh came from compounding alone.

How to use this calculator

Drag the sliders or type into the number boxes. The future value, total invested, and wealth gained update instantly. The donut shows the invested-vs-returns split; the area chart shows year-by-year growth so you can see when compounding starts dominating contributions (usually after year 8-10).

Tip: Try 10%, 12%, 14% return rates to see how sensitive the final corpus is to small changes in expected return. The difference between 10% and 14% over 20 years is staggering — that's why expense ratio and fund selection matter.

Frequently asked questions

Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund returns are market-linked. This calculator assumes a constant rate of return — real returns will vary year to year. Use it for planning, not as a promise.

What return rate should I assume?

For equity mutual funds, 11-13% CAGR is a reasonable long-term assumption. For hybrid funds, 9-11%. For debt funds, 6-8%. Conservative planners use 10% across the board.

Does the SIP calculator account for taxes and exit load?

No — this calculator shows gross returns. Equity mutual fund gains over ₹1.25 lakh per year are taxed at 12.5% LTCG (when held over a year). For an accurate after-tax view, use our Capital Gains Calculator.

Should I do SIP or lumpsum?

If you have a large amount sitting in cash and the market is at fair value, a phased lumpsum (STP over 6-12 months) often beats SIP. If you're saving from monthly income, SIP is the natural fit. Compare both using the Lumpsum Calculator.

What about step-up SIP — raising the SIP each year?

Stepping up your SIP by 10% each year (mirroring salary growth) can grow the final corpus by 60-80%. See our Step-up SIP Calculator.
