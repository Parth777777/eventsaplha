How the lumpsum formula works

FV = P × (1 + r)n

Where P is the principal, r is the annual rate (as a decimal — 12% = 0.12), and n is the number of years. Each year, the entire balance — principal plus all accumulated gains — earns the next year's return.

Lumpsum vs SIP — when which?

Lumpsum is better when you already have a sizeable amount in cash AND the market is at a fair-to-low valuation. SIP is better when you're investing from a monthly salary or when you're uncertain about market direction (rupee-cost averaging smooths the entry).

A useful middle path: park the lumpsum in a liquid fund, then run a 6-12 month STP (Systematic Transfer Plan) into your equity fund — captures lumpsum benefit if market dips, averages cost if market rises.

FAQ

How accurate is this for actual mutual fund returns?

It assumes a constant compound rate. Real funds have year-to-year volatility — over a 10-year horizon the long-run CAGR is usually close to the assumption, but interim drawdowns of 20-40% are normal.

Does this account for exit load and taxes?

No, this is a gross-of-tax calculator. For after-tax view, use the Capital Gains Tax Calculator.

Should I time the market for a lumpsum?

Historically, time-in-market beats timing-the-market for any horizon over 7 years. A simple rule: if Nifty PE is in the bottom third of its 10-year range, lump in; if top third, stagger via STP.
